
**Formal verification** is a method used to mathematically prove the correctness of a system's design with respect to its specifications. This technique employs formal methods, such as logic-based tools, model checking, and theorem proving, to ensure that the design adheres strictly to desired properties, making it especially valuable for verifying the reliability and security of critical systems.

### References for Definitions of Formal Verification:

1. **Huth, Michael, and Mark Ryan**. *Logic in Computer Science: Modelling and Reasoning about Systems*. Cambridge University Press, 2004.  
   - This text introduces formal verification as a method to use mathematical logic to establish the correctness of software and hardware systems.

2. **Clarke, Edmund M., Orna Grumberg, and Doron A. Peled**. *Model Checking*. MIT Press, 1999.  
   - This seminal book on model checking provides insights into how formal verification (particularly model checking) is used to exhaustively check whether a model of a system meets a given specification.

3. **Baier, Christel, and Joost-Pieter Katoen**. *Principles of Model Checking*. MIT Press, 2008.  
   - This text offers a detailed overview of formal verification, focusing on model checking and formal methods for verifying complex systems against their specifications.

These references provide a comprehensive view of formal verification, including both foundational and advanced concepts across different types of formal methods.
